The latest changes to the book, newest first. Each entry says what changed for a reader.

<Updates>
  <Update label="5 October 2026" tags="Simulations, Reader, Layout, Sound" description="Diagrams now show the mechanism; the reading edition keeps pictures and speaks the explanation.">

  - All 42 step-through diagrams now use causal scenes: bytes copy into buffers, calls follow changed routes, stacks push and pop, and geometric points project onto a viewport.
  - Reader editions keep compact diagram and table pictures. Narration skips dense visual labels, long numeric literals, and optional hover-card bodies. Register names are spaced for speech; voice selection stays chosen as voices load.
  - The toy CPU lab marks its next action, read source, copied byte, and changed registers. The pointer tracer now uses its lesson's capture and shows each calculation and read separately.
  - Compact controls, narrower table columns, plain section headings and navigation, and short margin explanations make the page calmer.
  - Original music and rain loops, plus gentle optional button sounds, start off. Play, pause, stop, and volume controls are in the reader settings.

  </Update>
  <Update label="3 October 2026" tags="New lessons, Game engines, Mobile, Progress, Hover cards, Reader mode" description="Two new lessons, more on how a game works, a calmer layout on phones, and ways to keep track of what you have read.">

  - **Two new lessons.** [4.2 How an Engine Orders and Shares Its Work](/pages/4/12/) follows how systems share data through resources and events, how one frame's work is ordered and run in parallel, and how an engine notices only what changed. [6.6 How a Game Reads Input](/pages/8/11/) follows a key press, a stick, and a mouse from the device to a game action.
  - **Some lesson numbers moved up by one.** In Chapter 4, lessons 4.2 to 4.12 are now 4.3 to 4.13, and in Chapter 6, lessons 6.6 to 6.9 are now 6.7 to 6.10, to make room for the two new ones. Lesson links did not change, and neither did the ticks you have already saved.
  - **More on how a game works, inside the lessons that need it.** Real time and game time, and why a long frame is clamped (4.1); extrapolation (4.7); local and world copies of a transform, engine axis conventions, several cameras, and texture coordinates (7.1); who decides whether an object is drawn, HDR, anti-aliasing, and the renderer's own copy of the world (7.2); how long an asset stays loaded and when a changed file is picked up (9.1); two ears and falloff (9.4); several windows (6.7).
  - **A calmer layout on phones.** The chat button steps out of the way while you scroll, long file names in a lab's source card no longer widen the page, "On this page" stays as one bar, and the listening controls take less room in landscape.
  - **Mark lessons and chapters done.** A tick appears beside each finished lesson in the lesson list, each chapter shows how many of its lessons are done, and the contents page and the home cards show the same progress. It is saved in your browser only.
  - **Hide either side panel from the panel itself.** The lesson list and "On this page" each have a Hide button, and a tab at the screen edge brings the panel back.
  - **Hover cards, used sparingly.** A glossary word shows its definition when you hover, focus, or tap it, but only where the book first uses the word, not every time it appears. A mention such as "Lesson 2.1" is now a link that shows what that lesson covers. Lessons can also write cards of their own: an example, a reference, a tip, an alternative, a recommendation, or a closer explanation, each labelled by kind. The Reader theme panel has a Hover cards On/Off choice if you would rather not see them.
  - **Reader mode keeps its controls on screen.** Back, play or pause, forward, stop, a speed button, and a way out of reader mode stay at the top while you scroll. Back and forward skip a paragraph, the speed button steps from 0.75× to 2× and is remembered, and on a phone the bar fits in two short rows.
  - **How games work.** New sections explain the parts of a game: the frame and its timing, entities and scenes, transforms and quaternions, 2D and 3D rendering, shaders, animation, picking, windows, interfaces, assets, glTF models, and audio. The [How games work](/how-games-work/) page maps each part to its section.
  - **Lesson components.** Steps, tabs, accordions, fields, maths, panels, worked examples, captions, link buttons, tooltips, and more are now used across the lessons and pages, wherever they help. The [components page](/components/) shows each one.
  - **A chat button** for asking questions about the book appears on every page. Its hint text names the lesson, chapter, or section you are reading, and it takes the chapter's colour. Move it to any corner, or turn it off, in the Reader theme panel.
